Description¶

The datasets we will be working with are both from the city of Chicago. They are ‘Current Employee Names, Salaries, and Position Titles’ (https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/current-employee-names-salaries-and-position-titles) and 'Budget - 2026 Budget Ordinance - Appropriations' (https://data.cityofchicago.org/Administration-Finance/Budget-2026-Budget-Ordinance-Appropriations/6694-f78c/data_preview). The goals for our project are to answer multiple questions with these datasets, focusing on the salary variability within and across different departments. We want to investigate if salary is greater within or across each department, if there are similar/same job titles in different departments that aren’t paid equally, what the range/mean/median of salaries are across each department, and how spread out the salaries are. We start off with loading and cleaning up the data, eventually merging both datasets. We then graph our results, investigate relationships, and state concrete conclusions.

Research Questions Addressed:¶

  1. Does salary vary more within departments or across different departments?
  2. Are similar or identical job titles paid differently depending on the department?
  3. What are the range, mean, and median salaries across each department?
  4. How spread out are salaries within each department?
  5. Is there a relationship between average salary and budget per employee?
  6. Does department size, measured by employee count, help determine total department budget?
  7. Can a department’s total budget be predicted using employee count, average salary, median salary, and salary spread?

Convert Hourly Wages to Annual Salary¶

Some employees are paid hourly. In order to make them comparable across employees, we estimate the annual salary using the equation:

Annual salary = Hourly Rate x Typical Hours x 52 Weeks

Once this is done, we further clean the data by dropping the typical hours column, as it will no longer be used.

In [5]:
#Convery hourly to annual salary using above equation
current.loc[
    (current['Salary or Hourly'] == 'HOURLY') & (current['Annual Salary'].isna()),
    'Annual Salary'
] = current['Hourly Rate'] * current['Typical Hours'] * 52

Model Building Outline¶

What will a department's budget be based on the number of employees and the average and median employee salary.

We will build a Multiple Linear Regression Model with the following parameters:

Target (Y): -total_budget

Predictor Variables:
-employee_count (this will be our primary predictor due to its strong relationship found in our analysis) -avg_salary -median_salary

Derived Features: -salary_std

From our analysis, we gathered that there was a positive relationship between employee count and total budget, showing that department size is a key feature in driving departmental spending. We also saw that salary variables had weak relationships with budget per employee, but potentially, when combined with other features, contribute to the prediction.

We chose a multiple linear regression model because total_budget is a continuous variable and the relationships between variables show some linearity. Ultimately, we want to quantify the impact of each of the predictor variables.

From our model, we expect the employee count to dominate.

Outline of Steps:

  1. Data selection and prep -Merge datasets -Select desired variables: -total_budget -employee_count -avg_salary -median_salary -salary_std -Handle missing values and ensure data types are correct and formatted
  2. Feature Scaling -Standardize the predictor variables
  3. Split into testing and training
  4. Fit a lienar regression model using the variables
  5. k-Fold Cross Validation -Apply k-fold cross-validation (k=10, likely) on the training set
  6. Model Evaluation Metrics -Evaluate performance by: -R^2 -RMSE -MAE
  7. Interpret the model -Find most influential predictors, confirming that employee_count dominates
  8. Could also compare this model to a k-Nearest Neighbors model
